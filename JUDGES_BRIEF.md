# EarthMimic: brief for the judges

## One sentence

EarthMimic finds the places on Earth that behave most like a landing site on the Moon or Mars,
plans a field trip through them, and states plainly what no place on Earth can reproduce.

## The problem

Before hardware or crews go to the Moon or Mars, they are rehearsed at "analog" sites on Earth.
Those sites are usually chosen by tradition and reputation. EarthMimic chooses them from data,
for a specific landing site and a specific mission, and shows its reasoning.

## How it works, in six steps

1. **Collect.** Measurements for 494 land locations on Earth and 13 landing sites
   (7 at the Moon's south pole, 6 on Mars) are downloaded from NASA archives and cached.
2. **Clean.** Bad values become missing values. Nothing is filled in or guessed, and every
   change is counted in a report.
3. **Pair.** A "relationship agent" decides which Moon or Mars measurement may be compared with
   which Earth measurement. A proposer suggests pairings; fixed checks decide (same physical
   quantity, convertible units, enough data, the column actually varies). 21 pairings are
   accepted and 1 is refused: elevation, which is measured from a different reference on each world.
4. **Score.** Every Earth location gets a match from 0 to 100% per property and overall.
   Properties Earth cannot reproduce are left out of the score instead of quietly lowering it.
5. **Route.** The best-matching locations are joined into a field trip of up to 5 stops.
   A stop is added only when it tests a new property or double-checks one at a second site.
6. **Stress-test.** Separately, Earth's extremes (coldest, driest, dustiest, most volcanic)
   are matched to each landing site as places to push equipment to failure.

## What the user sees

| Page | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `out/index.html` | **Home.** Which landing site? Headline numbers and one card per site |
| `out/explorer.html` | **Explorer.** How does one place compare on one property? One world map, one property at a time, one answer |
| `out/campaign_map.html` | **Field trip map.** Where do I go and what do I test there? The trip, each stop's matches, stress-test sites, NASA volcano and dust-storm records |
| `out/evidence.html` | **Evidence.** Can I trust this? Validation, limits, the pairing agent's log, data provenance, mission weights |
| `out/report.md` | The same results as a written report |

Each page answers one question, so nothing is shown twice.

## Data sources

| Source | What it gives |
|---|---|
| NASA POWER (MERRA-2, CERES) | Earth climate: temperature, wind, pressure, soil wetness, UV-B, precipitation, albedo, aerosols, soil temperature at 1 m |
| LRO Diviner Polar Resource Product | Moon: surface temperature, ice stability depth, slopes |
| LRO LOLA | Moon: surface albedo |
| MGS MOLA | Mars: topography, slope, relief, pressure estimate |
| MGS TES | Mars: surface albedo |
| Mars Odyssey GRS | Mars: water in the shallow subsurface |
| PDS lander archives: Viking 1, Phoenix, Curiosity REMS, InSight, Perseverance MEDA | Mars: air temperature, pressure, wind, dust, UV-B at Gale |
| NASA EONET | Earth: recorded volcanic eruptions (266 volcanoes) and dust storms (17) |
| Terrain Tiles on AWS Open Data | Earth: elevation, slope, roughness. Built mainly from NASA's SRTM |

Two inputs are not NASA measurements and are worth stating before a judge asks:
- **Land outline.** Natural Earth (public domain) decides which grid points are land and draws the
  dashboard map. It selects locations; it supplies no scored value.
- **Terrain tiles.** They blend SRTM with other open elevation models (GMTED and others) outside
  SRTM's coverage, mainly at high latitudes.

Three values are physical facts, not measurements, and are labelled as such: zero pressure on the
Moon, zero rainfall on both worlds, and Sun geometry computed from latitude and axial tilt.

| Layer | Tool |
|---|---|
| Language | Python 3 |
| Numerical work | NumPy, pandas |

| Development | Built with an AI coding assistant (Claude Code) under the team's direction |

Size: about 7,800 lines across 23 Python modules, the page templates and the tests.

## Results to quote

- **494** Earth locations compared against **13** landing sites on **21** paired properties.
- **4 of 10** analog sites from the scientific literature rank in our top 5%. Chance alone gives 0.5.
  The literature sites were never used to tune the system.
- **Every** testable property of every landing site is covered by a trip of 3 to 5 stops.
- Gale crater's best analog is the **Chajnantor Plateau** in the Atacama, found from the data alone.
- The dustiest place the system found is the **Bodele Depression** in Chad, which is the
  dustiest place on Earth.
- For Mars sites, Earth matches or exceeds the landing site on cold, wind, dryness and rough ground.
  It cannot stand in for thin air, day-night temperature swings, or lunar cold. Those need a chamber.

## What makes it different

- **It says what it cannot do.** Each property is marked testable, partly testable, not reproducible
  on Earth, or not measured yet, with the reason.
- **Every stop is justified.** The page states what each stop adds and how well it matches.
- **Every number is traceable** to a named dataset and version, with a fingerprint of each input file.
- **It is validated** against known analog sites and with 300 re-rankings under perturbed weights.
- **Two questions, kept apart.** "Where is it alike?" and "where is it harsh enough to break my
  equipment?" are answered separately, so robustness never inflates a similarity score.

## Honest limits

- Earth sites are a 5-degree grid plus 33 named sites, so a "site" is a region about 50 km across.
- Mars weather exists only where a lander stood. Arcadia Planitia has none; UV exists only at Gale.
- The Gale UV figure is an estimate from midday readings, about 25% uncertain.
- The lunar ice pairing compares a share of area with a share of the year, so it carries reduced confidence (0.6).
- Volcanic ground is shown from eruption records only. There is no mineral data on the targets yet.
- Trip distances are straight great-circle lines. Roads, permits and seasons are not modelled.
- The trip's stops do not yet change with the start point; only their order and the distance do.

## Likely questions

**Is the AI deciding the science?** No. A language model may suggest which columns to compare.
Fixed, written checks accept or refuse each suggestion, and the system runs without the model.

**How do you know the ranking is right?** Known analogs from the literature land near the top
at eight times the chance rate, and the top sites stay stable when weights are perturbed.

**Why are some overall matches only about 50%?** Because no single place on Earth resembles Mars
on everything. That is the finding, and it is why the answer is a trip, not a place.

**Can it take new landing sites or new data?** Yes. Add a row to the site list and rerun.
New measurements need one metadata entry each, and the pairing agent handles the rest.
